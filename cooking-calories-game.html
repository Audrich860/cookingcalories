Cooking Calories

A board game about teenage obesity. Every meal you cook adds calories, every activity burns some back, and your goal is to finish the day close to your character's target.

How to play

4 players. Each starts with 300 coins and picks one of two character cards, which sets their calorie goal.

  1. Get money and place teenagers. Collect 300 coins and send your teenager to a shop to choose ingredients.
  2. Buy ingredients. Prices differ by shop. If two players want the same ingredient, they compete with a trivia question.
  3. Make a dish. Use your ingredients to cook a menu card. Healthy dishes cost more and have fewer calories.
  4. Draw an activity card. Exercise burns calories, eating adds them.

After the last hour, your net calories (food minus exercise) are compared with your goal. A balanced diet needs all four nutrient groups.

Companion

Play a solo round of the game on screen. The program tracks coins and net calories for you.

Prototype assumptions: the goal covers the whole game, and success is within 100 kcal of it.

About

Cooking Calories is an interdisciplinary project on teenage obesity. It links biology (nutrient groups), maths (net calorie and energy calculations), economics (food prices), chemistry and physics.

The game is adapted from Rival Restaurants. Calorie values are rounded estimates. Add your team names, sources and a feedback email here.
